# Changelog

## 0.5.0 — November 27, 2026

- **License.** From this version, the data, charts and text are under CC BY-NC 4.0 with an additional permission: commercial use is free when every screen that uses the data shows the credit with both links (or the official badge or card), shows the check date and updates at least weekly, and is registered in a "Used by" issue. Scripts stay MIT. Versions up to 0.4.0 stay under CC BY 4.0. `LICENSE`, `CITATION.cff`, both READMEs, the website and a `license` block at the top of `chains.json`, `sources.json` and `network.json` say the same.
- **Data, API and embeds.** A data page lists the files, documents a JSON API (`data/chains/index.json` and one file per chain, schema `post-quantum-blockchains/chain@1`, with every field explained), and offers a live badge and a 360 × 112 card for every chain in English and Chinese, with one-click copy. Badges and cards are rebuilt from the data on every build and carry the credit. Charts and figures cite "this site's data (each entry links its primary source)" and link to the data page instead of naming a file.
- **Algorithm map by NIST family.** Rows are NIST's mathematical families (lattice, hash-based, multivariate, code-based, isogeny, MPC-in-the-Head, symmetric); colour is NIST status: final standard (FIPS 204 and 205, 2024-08-13), selected with the standard in development (FN-DSA, FIPS 206), stateful hash-based standard (SP 800-208, 2020-10-29), candidate in round 3 of the additional signatures (8 candidates; NIST expects the round to take about two years), publicly broken, or not submitted to NIST. Variants have a dashed outline. Raqcoin's Rainbow and UOV keys sit under multivariate. The method page adds a NIST status table with its sources.
- **Search.** The table search and "Check a coin" ignore case, spaces and hyphens, match aliases (BNB Chain, BSC and 币安链; Polygon, MATIC and POL; TON and Toncoin), the pinyin initials of Chinese names (ggb for 狗狗币) and names with one wrong letter (two from eight letters), and suggest the nearest names instead of an empty result.
- **Chinese names.** 18 chains get the names mainland China market sites use (比特币, 以太坊, 瑞波币, 波场, 莱特币, 狗狗币, 门罗币, 大零币, 艾达币 and others); brand names without a common Chinese name stay as they are. Chinese pages show "name + ticker" in the coin list. `scripts/lang_purity.py` runs in CI: English pages must have no Chinese, and Chinese pages may use Latin script only for names, tickers, standards and other proper nouns.
- **Home page.** The first screen keeps the answer, one line, "Check a coin" and three figures; how the numbers are counted folds below. Filters are lighter, with counts as small badges, and only scope and stage show until "More filters" is opened. Every list that expands folds back to where the reader was, and Back from a chain page restores the filters, "show all" and the scroll position. "Submit a project" sits on the home, method and data pages.
- **X posts.** `x_talk` (dated posts by people or official accounts with a public identity, with their role around the chain, the post's type (plan or timeline, proposal, release, research or statement), a one-sentence summary in each language, the link and the day we opened it), with validation. They show on chain pages (the three newest, the rest behind "Show more") and on the weekly changes page, as claims that never set a stage, and only when there is checked data. Questions from ordinary holders are not published.
- **Replies.** Submissions and corrections get an automatic reply at once, a maintainer's reply within 2 days and a decision usually within 3 days. The reply that closes an accepted submission gives the chain's page and its badge code (`scripts/triage.py --approved <id>`).
- **Stage changes, 2026-11-28.** Kaspa moves from stage 0 to 1: a core developer opened KIP-22 "Pay-to-Public-Key-Hash via P2SH" (kaspanet/kips pull request #48, Draft, not merged); it hides keys only, so Kaspa can reach stage 2 at most on it, and the page tells it apart from the older, different "KIP22" pull request #37. Cronos moves from stage 0 to 1: pull request #2263 in its own repository upgrades to Cosmos SDK v0.55.1 and CometBFT v0.40 for v1.9 and tests ML-DSA-65 validator keys; it is not merged or released, and accounts stay secp256k1. Cosmos Hub stays at stage 1, with the SDK v0.55.0 release date corrected to 2026-07-28. Ethereum Classic stays at stage 0; skinnyCAT, a third-party unaudited demo contract that verifies leanSPHINCS signatures on mainnet, is on its timeline without changing the stage. Bitcoin adds the SHRINCS draft BIP posted to bitcoindev (no BIP number) and Ethereum adds EIP-8288 (Draft); neither changes a stage.
- **Browser tests.** A Playwright run in CI (`tests/e2e`, `.github/workflows/e2e.yml`) clicks every control on every page type in English and Chinese, on a desktop screen and a phone, including keyboard use, Back and Forward, the language switch and links that open with filters, and reports a page × control table.
- A stage tracker website, in English and Chinese: https://balance0014.github.io/post-quantum-blockchains/. Each chain has its own page. It opens with one dated sentence that gives the stage, the reason for it and a link to the source, followed by dated milestones, all sources and network status. Every label has a definition on hover or tap, and the method page publishes the rules, thresholds, data fields, limits and how to cite. `scripts/build_site.py` builds it from the data, and GitHub Actions publishes it.
- The home page reads in layers: an overview (the answer, four headline figures, the largest chains by stage, a running total of post-quantum mainnet launches and an algorithm map), five questions a holder asks (usable today, closest to mainnet, moving now, no post-quantum progress in a year, worth a closer look), each card stating its rule and opening the table filtered to the same chains, then the full table. The table has one column per dimension: stage, algorithm with its standard status and NIST security level, coverage (new accounts and how existing coins move), the post-quantum mainnet start date, and the latest post-quantum progress with a count for the last 90 days. It filters by scope, stage, design, standard status and coverage (algorithm family, migration, audit and network status under More filters), searches by name, and sorts from a menu. Every filter option has a definition. Filters live in the address, so a filtered view can be shared, and `/` jumps to the search box. Below the table come a timeline (how long each chain has run post-quantum signatures on mainnet, milestones by year, month or week, and launches by year), a short version of the method, data downloads and public contact channels. Every chart has a title, a source and date line, a CSV download, and values on hover or tap. Motion is limited to short transitions that respect reduced-motion settings. The site follows the system's light or dark theme and has a switch, and switching language keeps the reader's place.
- Chain pages follow the same order: the answer, then algorithm (standard status, security level, hybrid or not, reference sizes, audit), coverage (new accounts, how existing coins move, coins behind exposed public keys where research has published a figure), progress, evidence and background (network status, code activity, market cap). A missing page shows the site header and a search that opens the table.
- Three researched fields in `data/chains.json`, each with its own source and grade: `migration` (stage 2 and up: native, available, testnet, planned, none or unknown), `audit` (stage 3 and up: audited, partial, none found or unknown; only a report by an outside firm whose scope covers the post-quantum signature code counts), and `exposure` (coins behind public keys already visible on chain, only where published research gives a figure: Bitcoin and Ethereum).
- Code activity counts commits to the main repository's default branch in 90 days and leaves out authors whose name ends in `[bot]`; it is background and never sets a stage. The site no longer counts commits by keyword: the method page explains why.
- Chains at stage 4 or 5 can record `pq_mainnet_since` in `data/chains.json`: the day post-quantum signatures became usable on the current mainnet (the genesis block for post-quantum native chains, the upgrade's activation for the others), with its own source. 23 of the 26 chains at stage 4 or 5 have one; Starknet and Minima are shown as not yet sourced rather than guessed, and Dilithion's sources disagree on the day (2026-01-28, 2026-01-18 and 2025-01-18), so none is picked.
- The website serves the data for download (`data/chains.csv`, `chains.json`, `network.json`, `projects.json`, `sources.json`) and an `llms.txt` for AI assistants, rebuilt from `data/` on every build.
- `data/chains.json` rates chains from stage 0 to 5 under the rules in [docs/stages.md](docs/stages.md): every CoinGecko top-100 coin that has its own network (layer 1 or layer 2), older chains with a mainnet opt-in, and post-quantum native and early projects. Top-100 coins without their own network are listed with the reason, and `scripts/rank.py` fails CI when a top-100 coin is in neither list.
- Eight new projects in `projects.json`: Splendor Quantum, Qbit, Discrete, Dilithion, Parano1d, Aere Network, QoreChain and Minima. Existing project entries are unchanged.
- A weekly patrol (`scripts/patrol.py`) reads each chain's newest block from its own RPC or explorer, its latest release and its commits in 90 days, checks every source link, and proposes the results in a pull request that a person reviews. It never changes a stage.
- New CI checks: the chain schema and evidence rules (stage 3 and up needs primary evidence; a feature described only in a project's own documents is capped at stage 2), matching numbers in English and Chinese, banned words, every source link opens (`scripts/check_links.py`), every link inside the website resolves, and the share image shows current figures.
- Corrections in `chains.json`, which differ from `projects.json` (not edited in this release): QANplatform is rated 2, because only its documentation describes post-quantum signing on the testnet; Quantus accepts both ML-DSA-87 and ML-DSA-65; QuantumCoin is also labelled "NIST family" for its pre-standard hybrid, which is still accepted.
- Corrections after an independent review of every entry. Litecoin moves from stage 0 to 1: LIP-0003, the MWEB specification, only mentions a later ElGamal switch for amount commitments against quantum computers, which is not a quantum-related protocol change, and a core maintainer said in September 2026 that the team is looking into it. Nexus, Nervos CKB, Raqcoin, Tidecoin, Abelian and Mochimo get sourced mainnet milestones; Neptune Cash records its 2025 relaunch and its 2026 pause; qtcchain records its first mainnet and the restart with a 2,000,000 QTC treasury; Starknet names the author of each Falcon account class and that none is audited. Wording was tightened for Aere, Parano1d, Quantus, QuantumCoin, Cellframe, BNB Chain, Pearl, Zcash, Bitcoin, Raqcoin and QANplatform.
- After acceptance review: standard status is given per scheme, and variants of NIST schemes get their own status, "variant, version not reviewed"; "publicly broken" is a flag, not a status. Reference sizes appear only for the parameter sets a chain names. "Hybrid" is defined and reads "not applicable" until a scheme is deployed. The home page says who it is for, answers for Bitcoin and Ethereum, adds a coin search, a line that reconciles every headline count, and a citation strip with BibTeX; the table shows 20 rows before "show all". Chain pages add a "for holders" block and a report or appeal button; the contact section lists what it takes to be listed or re-rated, links the public correction log, and says who runs the site and how it is funded. Milestones unrelated to a chain's signatures (KCC's HTTPS gateway, Hedera's TLS and hashing items, Kaspa KIP-16, Monero MRL #151) are removed.
- Rule changes after the second data review (in [docs/stages.md](docs/stages.md)): stage 1 needs research, a roadmap, a proposal or a prototype from the core developers, the foundation or the main client teams, and outside contributors' proposals stay on the timeline only (Kaspa stays at 0, Polkadot at 1); stage 2 also counts a formal protocol specification published by the core team when a chain has no public proposal process, and a passing mention inside a proposal about something else does not count (Litecoin moves to 1). "Post-quantum progress" has one definition with a list of what counts and what does not; entries that do not count carry `progress: false`, are greyed on the timeline and are left out of "latest" and the 90-day count, which are recomputed for every chain. "Design" has three values: post-quantum native, added later (stage 3 and up) and not yet. Security level and hybrid signing read "not applicable" before a scheme is deployed. Stage 4 and 5 ratings show where the evidence stops: own construction, variant not reviewed, no audit found, or no independent network data. A neutral `supply` note records premines and treasury or developer shares (qtcchain, Parano1d, Splendor Quantum). Data corrections from the same review: Nervos CKB is SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) with a partial audit (the SPHINCS+ library is not in the audited files); Bittensor's Falcon is the original, not FN-DSA; Aere's security level counts only its ML-DSA transaction keys; QuantumCoin's hybrid switch is dated by its block (2026-02-03); Sui testnet runs protocol 144; QAN's documentation names ML-DSA-65 (added 2026-02-27); QoreChain's hybrid requirement is stated as of 2026-11-27; Neptune's elliptic-curve use is limited to one address type's payment notifications; Dogecoin records libdogecoin PR #294 (Raccoon-G); xCoin and Soqucoin get their mainnet launches as milestones. Cardano's CIP-0197 and CIP-0206 stay on the timeline but are not counted as progress: CIP-0206 comes from an outside company, and CIP-0197's author could not be confirmed as a member of the core teams. Bittensor's mainnet switch is confirmed from an archive node (unset at block 9,484,618, on at block 9,484,619, 2026-11-11) and recorded as a milestone. On the chain page, "For holders" opens with the same answer for every stage, and technical detail moves to the algorithm block. A chain with no mainnet carries the label "Early · testnet only" at the top of its page, as in the home page's groups; its design is stated in the algorithm block.
- The home page is prerendered and kept as is in the browser instead of being built again, lists the first 20 chains in its HTML, and carries only its own language's text, so it is about 40% smaller; the stylesheet is minified and web fonts no longer shift the layout when they load. Each page inlines only the style rules its first screen uses and loads the full stylesheet without blocking the first paint; sections far below the first screen are laid out only when they come near, and the table measures its clipped text only then. The Lighthouse check serves the site compressed, as GitHub Pages does (`scripts/serve_site.py`), and takes the median of three runs per page.
- Every date is the day the event happened, in UTC. Entries that record our own checks are marked "checked" and left out of the monthly chart, and CI requires every entry dated on a review day to say which it is. Chain pages say when network data comes from the project's own node or a community explorer.
- The patrol reads every block endpoint two or three times and keeps the highest block, recording when it read it and every height it saw: QANplatform's public testnet RPC sits behind a load balancer, and one backend still answers with a block from September. A chain is marked stopped only when an independent explorer (`network.check`) agrees; with one source it is marked could not check, which moves Bitway from stopped to could not check.
- A golden regression check: ten facts checked by hand against pinned primary sources (`data/golden.json`, `scripts/golden.py`). CI fails if the data stops stating one of them, and the links job confirms that each pinned source still shows it.
- Five source links that no longer open, or close the connection for automated clients, now point to archived copies of the same pages: Abelian milestones, the Neptune Cash genesis block and three Cellframe pages.

## 0.4.0 — November 27, 2026

- The repository is now a continuously maintained map rather than an October 23 snapshot. The READMEs open with what it is, separate chains and projects from resources, and state what gets listed and how quickly submissions are reviewed (within 7 days).
- Both charts are now drawn from the data by `scripts/charts.py` as SVG, with a "data as of" date: a deployment timeline and an algorithm map with a log-scale size comparison. They replace the hand-drawn October 23 PNGs, which remain in the git history.
- The summary counts at the top of both READMEs are generated from the data too. CI regenerates the charts, tables and summaries, fails on any difference, and now runs Python 3.9 and 3.12 on Ubuntu 24.04.
- Listed projects can link back to their entry with a [badge](CONTRIBUTING.md#listed-badge).
- The resource list has a section for papers. The methodology explains how the charts sort projects by deployment status.
- No project, source or size data changed.
